Main page layouts, round 2

Built from what the repo needs the page to do. It has to start and stop a run (benchmark, generations, problems, Grow, Stop, new benchmark), show the agents (one solver per problem plus the proposer), show the lineage (change, what was shed, signal, score, kept or pruned) and let you open one version (diff, signal, harness, activity).

It also has to hold up as a 3-minute demo, follow the hackathon rule that the tree is the largest thing and the numbers stay small, and handle five trees (chess, legal, health, finance, coding) once BENCHMARKS.md is done. Trees are the live demo; all text is placeholder.

1 · Grove

One small bonsai per benchmark, standing side by side on one long shelf. Under each, its name and held-out score. The one that is running breathes and shows its agents as a line of dots under its pot. Click a tree and the page becomes option 2 for that tree.

Why: BENCHMARKS.md plans five trees running at once. This is the only layout that shows all of them, and "five self-improving agents in five domains" is a strong opening shot for the demo.

2 · One tree, ledger in the margin

The tree takes the left two thirds. The right margin is a ledger with no boxes: the score, then every version as a ruled entry (change, what it shed, signal, score), newest first, cut ones struck in red. Click an entry and the margin turns into that version: diff, signal, harness. The ask bar is one sentence at the top; agents show as a single status line under it.

Why: closest to DESIGN.md (tree largest, numbers small beside it, no cards) and it keeps every control and every field the page has today, only rearranged.

3 · Version open

What option 2 looks like after clicking a version. The tree stays, the clicked branch is lit and the rest dims. The margin holds the story of that one change in order: what failed (signal), what it changed (diff, green and red lines), what it shed, and the verdict with the score before and after. Raindrop trace link at the bottom.

Why: this is the "click a branch" moment from the brief, and it is where judges see that changes are real edits to a real harness.

4 · Run in progress

While a run is going, the page centres on the tree and the agents gather around the pot: one small mark per solver (filling as games finish, green won, red lost), the proposer above the tree with the sentence it is writing. The shoot being tested grows pale at the same pace. When the verdict lands the marks clear and the page returns to option 2.

Why: the agents are the most alive data the page has. Today they sit in a list; here you can see eight Haiku players and one Opus proposer working on the tree.

5 · Before and after

The tree on the left. On the right, proof: the same chess opening played by the seed and by the best version, two boards side by side with the move where they diverge, and the result under each. For question sets the boards become the same question answered both ways. The score sits between them.

Why: the repo already saves replays (tasks/chess/_replays, replay.html). A score going from 20% to 48% is abstract; a game the seed lost and the best version holds is not.

6 · Stage (demo mode)

For the 3 minutes on stage. Full-screen tree, nothing else but one large caption that tells the story from the real lineage, one step per key press: the seed, what it failed, the change, the verdict, and the growth. The tree acts out each step (the shoot grows, the cut falls). Press D to leave stage mode and get the working page back.

Why: the submission is a live demo. A page built for running is not the same as a page built for telling; this gives you both from the same data.
